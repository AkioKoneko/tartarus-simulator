How to vault Rainbows

A rough strategy to learn before your first run. It comes from over 25 million simulated 20-day drills, played by a rules-based strategy that was tuned until it stopped improving.

The default drill: tools at the first Supreme and a Warden's Cache gift per Rainbow. 1,000,000 runs, margins under ±0.002.

The short version

  1. Devour every day you can.Waiting to build a bigger chain lost Rainbows: 0.57 per run with a small threshold and 0.38 with a large one, against 0.66 when you always devour (no tools).
  2. Swap the warden's low-tier inmate every day.Normal and Magic inmates, and Rare too once you hold tools. A fresh roll is worth more than either on average. Never swapping drops results to 0.36 per run, and swapping only Normals gives 0.61.
  3. Accept Chain reactions.Opening every door on the next walk floods the block with material: 0.66 per run against 0.53 when skipped.
  4. Accept an Execution only when it gains more than it removes.Count what it would wipe out. Always accepting gave 0.64 and never accepting 0.61, against 0.66 when you check first.
  5. Keep opening doors, and walk far.New inmates are the raw material, and farther destinations reach fresh doors sooner (0.66 against 0.64 for short walks).
  6. Stock each tier with a Rat, a Mammoth, and a Lion or Wolf.Then any beast the eater re-rolls into finds its next meal, so the chain can't stall at that tier.
  7. Take Hangman's Rope or Avenger's Box when offered, and skip the Writhing Iron Box.Each of the top two more than doubled a run's Rainbows in testing. The Iron Box was the only tool that made things worse.
  8. At the Warden's Cache, take what fits the board.Judging each gift on the spot beat always taking the same one. If you want a default, Crimson Wine.

A day on the block

Every day runs in the same order. Here is what the strategy does at each step.

  1. Patrol

    Pick one of the three destinations. A locked door usually wins: it adds a new inmate, who may devour today from the warden's cell. Go to a known inmate instead when it has a strong devour waiting. A door with an event badge is a bonus, especially a Chain reaction. Prefer the farther destination when they're close, and don't open a rat nest before it hatches.

  2. Event

    Accept a Chain reaction. For an Execution, count the inmates it would remove against the tiers the executioner gains, and accept only if you come out ahead.

  3. Devour

    If the warden's inmate has a target, devour. Pick the first meal whose chain is worth the most: the Trainer shows each option's chain odds. After that bite the chain runs by itself and can't be stopped.

  4. Swap

    If the warden's inmate is Normal or Magic (or Rare with tools), swap it. When it has nothing to eat, swap before the devour: the new inmate might have a target today.

  5. End the day

    Once nothing in the warden's cell can gain, end the day. The next morning, prey marks grow, nests hatch, and new destinations are drawn.

Chains: how many beasts you need

After every bite the eater rises a tier and re-rolls into a random beast. The chain continues if that new beast has something to eat at its new tier. So what matters is which beasts are waiting at each tier above the eater. Wolves eat only Rats and Rats eat only Mammoths, so those two are the beasts you can't do without.

Chance the chain finds its next meal at a tier

Standard food chain. In hard mode's loop chain every beast has exactly one meal, so each beast covers 25% and only all four guarantee the next bite.

Hard mode

With the loop food chain (Mammoth eats Lion, Lion eats Wolf, Wolf eats Rat, Rat eats Mammoth), every beast has a single meal. Chains break far more often: the strategy makes 0.27 Rainbows per run with no tools and 0.71 with tools, against 0.66 and 1.39 on the standard chain. The same habits still win. Expect fewer chains, and keep all four beasts at the tiers you're building toward.

Practice it

Start a Rules drill and press Trainer in the top bar. It recommends a move for every decision and marks it on the board. It checks the move with hundreds of live playouts from your exact position, and grades each move you make, so you can see where your play differs from these numbers.

Open the simulator

How these numbers were made. Every run used the simulator's own rules engine. A rules-based strategy played them, making each choice from what a player can see. There's no AI involved: it's a set of scoring rules whose weights were tuned by trying each value and keeping whichever vaulted more Rainbows. Final comparisons used 1,000,000 runs each and tuning comparisons 40,000 to 150,000. Training then added 25.9 million more runs, scored on fresh prisons. The rules follow the season's video reveal. Odds and limits are practice values and may differ from the live game: new inmates roll 52% Normal, 27% Magic, 15% Rare and 6% Legendary, 18% of cells hide an event, and you get one devour and one swap a day over a 20-day drill. The full tables are in the strategy lab's notes.
