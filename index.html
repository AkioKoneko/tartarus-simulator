        How the block works

        The ring

        Sixteen cells form a ring, numbered clockwise from 01 in the top-left corner: 01 to 06 across the top, 07 to 09 down the right, 10 to 14 back along the bottom and 15 to 16 up the left. Cell 16 sits next to cell 01. The lantern marks where the warden stands. You start at cell 01.

        A day on the block

        1. Patrol. Each morning offers three destination cells from the next five clockwise from the warden, or two while you hold the Burning Stake. They glow on the ring and appear as cards under it. A destination can be a locked cell, which opens when the warden arrives, or a cell whose inmate you already know. You must patrol before anything else.
        2. No scouting. As in live, you can't see inside a locked cell before you open it. The Lab can allow scouting for practice, and in the contract challenge you can pay 1 voucher to scout a destination.
        3. Devour. Once a day, after the patrol, and only the inmate in the warden's cell can start it. Click a highlighted target. If the eater then has another valid target, a chain devour starts: pick its next meal, or stop the chain. It repeats while targets remain.
        4. Swap. Once a day, replace an inmate with a brand-new random one. It can come out stronger or weaker.
        5. End the day. The next morning, prey marks grow, ripe nests hatch, and new destinations are drawn.

        When an inmate is devoured, swapped away or sent off, its cell seals again. The next time that door opens, someone new is inside.

        The food chain

        Six relations, and both sides must share a rarity. Mammoth eats Lion and Wolf. Lion eats Wolf and Rat. Wolf eats Rat. Rat eats Mammoth. A Mammoth cannot eat a Rat, and no beast eats its own kind unless you hold the Rat-Head Skewer, which lets Rats eat Rats.

        The hard mode "Harder food chain" switch uses a simple loop instead: Mammoth eats Lion, Lion eats Wolf, Wolf eats Rat, and Rat eats Mammoth. Each beast then has exactly one meal. It's uncertain which version the live game uses, so you can practice both.

        Rarity

        Normal, Magic, Rare, Legendary, Supreme, Rainbow. A devour raises the eater one tier, then re-rolls its beast, so check its new matchups before the next meal. An inmate that reaches Rainbow leaves its cell for the vault and adds a Warden's Cache claim. You can keep raising more Rainbows for the rest of the run.

        Special events

        Some sealed doors carry an event badge. When your patrol opens one, the event appears and you choose to accept or skip it. Doors opened any other way use up their event without triggering it.

        Chain reaction
        Your next patrol walks clockwise from the warden to the chosen destination and opens every sealed door on the way.
        Execution
        The inmate in the opened cell removes every inmate on the block that it can eat and that has a lower rarity. It gains one tier for each.

        Markers

        Ivory
        Belongs to a cell. Swap that cell's inmate and four random inmates on the block each gain a tier. The mark stays until the cell empties.
        Prey
        A Lion marks a same-rarity inmate it can eat. On each of the next three mornings both gain a tier. The mark ends if either leaves the block or changes beast.
        Scratch
        A scratched door shows the rarity inside. A Wolf of that rarity can devour the occupant through the door, whatever its beast, and gains +3.
        Rat nest
        A sealed nest hatches a Rare Rat on the morning two days after it appears. Opening the door before then destroys it.

        Tools and the Warden's Cache

        Your first Supreme earns a tool: pick one of three, and it lasts for the rest of the run. Each Rainbow that reaches the vault opens the Warden's Cache: pick one gift, which takes effect at once. Both work in drills and in the contract challenge. In the Lab you can start drills with random tools, or switch rewards off for pure rules practice.

        Contract challenge

        Twelve days with 6 dispatch vouchers and a starting kit. Each morning one patrol option is the nearest open cell, which lets you walk without adding inmates. Scouting costs 1 voucher, once a day: it shows the full occupant of one destination, and that day you can then only go there or to an open cell.

        Three contracts fall due at the end of days 4, 8 and 12. Ending a due day opens the delivery window: pick inmates (vault Rainbows count) that meet the contract, choose an escort, and send them, or skip the contract. A light escort costs 1 voucher and carries up to 36 pressure; a heavy escort costs 3 and carries up to 72. Each inmate scores 2 to the power of its tier. Each of your first two deliveries earns a refit tool; you may refresh the offer once for 1 voucher. From day 9, once the first two contracts are settled, you can deliver the heavy contract early, which ends the run. You pass by delivering at least two contracts, including the heavy one.

        Practice values

        The live game's exact odds, daily limits and some timing details are not public. This simulator uses stated practice values for them. You can change the drill values in the Lab; the contract challenge always uses fixed values. Riots, followers, loot and combat are not simulated.
